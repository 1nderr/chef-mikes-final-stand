Finding 1 · Highest leverage

Every power-up is somebody’s favourite and somebody’s dud

You already clocked this in your reply to darkfenix — “I’ve heard every variation of useless/useful for each of them.” The chart says you read it right. All three items drew both praise and dismissal, and no item is one-sided.

The honest caveat first: these are five, five and three mentions. Nets of +3 / +1 / +1 are far too thin to rank the items against each other, and you should not tune off them. What is robust is the shape — the presence of a red arm on every row. That pattern held across 47 independent players, and it is not what a merely mistuned item looks like. A weak item gets consistently ignored. These got argued about.

The cause is in the damage numbers, and it is very specific.

That flagged cell is the whole story of the popcorn complaints. The bomb deals 2. Reds have 3. So a bomb thrown into a Tanks wave detonates with full screenshake, a 40px radius and a satisfying noise — and every enemy in the blast walks out alive. That is almost certainly what GoodSoop meant by “if the popcorn worked I would have beat it,” and why HyperStride never used it twice. The item is not underpowered; it is correct against Rush (nine 1 HP whites) and reads as literally broken against its visual opposite. One bad first impression is enough to retire it for the rest of the run.

The same logic explains the other two. The pizza pierces — disc.gd never frees the projectile on hit — and _spawn_column() deliberately lines a tank wave up along the player’s axis, so one well-aimed throw can delete all four. Thrown into a scattered rush it gets one kill. That is a four-fold swing in value decided entirely by aim, which is exactly the gap between careeoki (“slicing through the tanks is satisfying”) and darkfenix (“didn’t find much use”). The icecream deals no damage at all; its worth is however many kills you convert during five seconds of half-speed. Played offensively it is the strongest thing in the game (darkfenix: “OP”); popped in a panic when already surrounded it does nothing visible (Radace: “underpowered”). Two people describing the same five seconds.

Note the cost ordering, because it sharpens Radace’s complaint considerably: the icecream is the most expensive thing on the menu and the only one that cannot kill anything. Ten seconds of clock for a utility effect is a hard sell unless the player already understands how to cash it in. That is a legibility problem before it is a balance problem.

Finding 2 · The root cause

Your cleverest system is invisible to every single player

0/47
comments show any awareness that the game chooses its waves based on what you are holding.

WaveDirector._decide_wave() reads your inventory and sends the wave you are equipped for: hold only discs and it sends Tanks; hold only bombs and it sends Rush; hold both or neither and it flips a coin. On top of that, _on_cadence_timeout() refuses to fire a wave at all unless four conditions hold — more than 18 enemies left, at least a third of the round already cleared, the microwave flash more than three seconds away, and the player holding at least one power-up.

That is a genuinely thoughtful piece of design. It means the game never interrupts your skill check, never opens with a set-piece, and never throws a formation at someone with an empty inventory. And not one person noticed.

This is the root cause sitting underneath Finding 1. Players graded each item against whatever wave happened to be on screen, without knowing their own inventory had summoned it. cannolicat’s “always hold on to an ice cream and a popcorn” is somebody who stumbled into manipulating the director — holding only bombs guarantees rush waves — and who has no idea the director exists. Meanwhile microwave_panel.gd forbids cooking the same item twice in a row, so players are forced to keep stocking an item they have already written off, which hardens the impression rather than challenging it.

Surfacing the counter relationship is the single highest-leverage change available, because it is not new work — the system already exists and already behaves well. It just never tells anyone. Naming the counter on the selection screen (“Pizza — slices through columns” / “Popcorn — clears crowds, won’t kill reds”) would likely collapse most of the balance contradiction on its own, because players would stop grading each item on the wrong wave.

The whole set

What people chose to talk about

Two things to pull out of this ranking.

First, the microwave concept is your strongest asset by a clear margin — it out-mentions even the art. Fourteen people went out of their way to say the idea was original, relatable or funny, and only Jaib2203 dissented (“nothing super creative in terms of gameplay”). Whatever you build next, that is the thing that earned the attention.

Second, a discount to apply honestly: music is the fourth-most-praised element and it is Tallbeard’s track, not yours. Nine people loved the soundtrack. Keep that in perspective when you read your own scorecard — the art, the concept and the game feel are the rows that reflect your work. The “strong for a first jam” row deserves a similar asterisk: nine people explicitly calibrated their praise to first-timer expectations.

Finding 3 · Retention

Difficulty is the top complaint, but mostly as a compliment

Read the wording, not the keyword. “Difficult but satisfying,” “lowkey difficult,” “hard too” — eight of the ten are compliments. The arcade feel is landing. Only two treat it as a defect, and they are the two that matter: darkfenix asked for tuning “to be less punishing,” and Ninkolas identified something structural.

The players read your curve accurately. Danilo Segrt reported it “got really hard at around 60 enemies,” which lands mid-band with the interval already compressed and the wave director firing. Dilemma 1253 got to “20 guys left” — which is two enemies short of the point where WaveDirector switches itself off. They died at the peak, with the game about to become easier. That is a bleak little detail, and it argues for the last stretch easing off less abruptly rather than for a flat difficulty cut.

Ninkolas is the most valuable comment in the set because it describes a structural failure rather than a tuning one: cleared 150, needed several attempts at 175, reached 200, “allllmost beat it,” and stopped — “I don’t quite have the patience to keep trying to get further.”

Here is the mechanism. enemies_total ratchets up by 25 on a win and never comes back down. Losing at 200 puts you straight back at 200, from 3 HP, with a two-hundred-kill run in front of you and no partial credit for the 180 you just managed. Every failure costs the entire run. That is not difficulty — difficulty is what the other eight commenters were enjoying — it is an absence of any relief valve, and it is what converts an engaged player who has won twice into someone who closes the tab.

Worth noting how cheap the fix is relative to the payoff: a step back down on death, or credit for enemies cleared, or simply letting the player retry the round they lost rather than the ladder they earned.

Finding 4 · The theme hook

The countdown moment is under-signalled where players are looking

Only three people raised juice and signalling, but lueegue’s is the sharpest strategic note anyone left: the microwave flash is your theme tie-in, players hang their whole run on it, and it deserves “ALL THE POSIBLE JUICE.” You agreed in your reply. There is a concrete spatial reason it under-delivers.

The microwave sits at (0, -100) — the top of a 256×224 screen — while the enemies converge on the player below it. The only cues that the grab window has opened are a sprite animation swap and a hum pitch tween, both local to a sprite the player is not looking at during a swarm. The cue is entirely in the wrong part of the screen.

Note the counterpoint from geo821: the skill check “wasn’t that hard to get most of the time.” The 1.25s window is generous and nobody complained about its length. So this is a request for more feedback, not more difficulty — a screen-level cue (a vignette, an edge pulse, the HUD timer doing more than flashing yellow) rather than a tighter window.

The two related requests are similarly cheap. Herobrine wants screenshake on kill; you currently shake only on bomb throw and on taking damage. Ninkolas wants an edge marker showing which side a wave is arriving from — and _spawn_column() already picks and knows origin, so the information exists and simply never reaches wave_warning.gd, which announces Tanks and Rush at top-centre with no direction attached.

Worth naming

Two non-trends, and the quiet win

Only one person asked for movement. A stationary turret is the most unusual choice in the game, and out of 47 comments exactly one — Frizja — wanted the player to move “at least a little bit.” Set against chriscollins84 explicitly praising the scheme (“the auto-fire and aiming is satisfying to control and easy to play without reading instructions”) and nine mentions of game feel overall, the design is working. Do not over-index on it.

Nobody reported a bug. Across the whole set there is no crash, no softlock, no broken state. The only comment that sounds like one — GoodSoop’s “if the popcorn worked” — is the damage-threshold legibility issue from Finding 1, not a defect. For a jam build assembled under deadline, shipping 48 comments deep with a clean bug sheet is the quiet win in this data.

The two remaining small items are worth a few minutes each: IdanR cannot read the instruction text and suggests a different colour or a mask behind it, and HyperStride could not tell the popcorn from “a chicken bucket.” Both are trivial fixes, and both directly caused a mechanic to be misused — HyperStride never worked out what the item was, then concluded it was underpowered.

Ranked by leverage

Where the effort pays

Ordered by expected impact per hour of work, not by how often each was requested — the two are quite different here.

  1. Make the wave counter-relationship visible

    The system already exists and already behaves well. Name each item’s counter on the selection screen — explicitly including that popcorn will not kill reds. This dissolves most of the balance contradiction without touching a single damage value.

  2. Stop restarting escalated players at the full escalated total

    Step the ladder down on death, or bank partial progress. This is the difference between Ninkolas playing a fourth round and Ninkolas closing the tab, and it is the only finding here that is costing you engaged players who have already won twice.

  3. Move the microwave flash cue to the screen level

    Your theme hook currently signals itself in the one corner of the screen a swarmed player is not watching. Keep the 1.25s window — it is generous and nobody asked for more time. Add the vignette, the edge pulse, the HUD escalation.

  4. Fix the two readability items

    A mask behind the instruction text, and a popcorn sprite that reads as popcorn. Minutes of work, and each one is currently costing a mechanic its first impression.

  5. Add the directional wave marker

    _spawn_column() already knows the origin. Passing it through to wave_warning.gd turns a telegraph the player cannot act on into one they can.
